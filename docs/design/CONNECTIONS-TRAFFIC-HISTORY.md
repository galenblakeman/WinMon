# Connections rows, traffic history and pins

Connections appears immediately before Usage. The grid shows one row per executable path, with exact process-lifetime fallback for unavailable paths. There is no display-mode selector or endpoint expansion. The action bar matches the 54-DIP minimum of Processes and Services, with a name and one summary line. Show cumulative totals there and keep the live rates in the table; full traffic details remain in its tooltip and Properties. Properties and copied details retain every endpoint, protocol, state and owner PID.

Reuse the Services history-column layout and graph renderer for combined receive/send traffic on a shared two-minute scale. Capture uses retained observations through the selected sample; missing rates remain gaps. Endpoint and traffic observations are not restart-persisted.

Reuse ProcessPins for independent Connections pins: executable identities when paths are available, exact lifetimes otherwise. Pin buttons use the existing filled/unfilled icons and accent, localized accessible names, and keyboard activation. When the exact lifetime’s path becomes available, promote its pin to the executable identity and merge duplicates in original order. Pins stay in insertion order above ranked rows, outside Top. Search, App only and Pinned only apply consistently. Persist pin metadata and the filter in MonitorPreferences. Disconnected, unavailable and unrecorded pins remain labeled without stale endpoint data; retained traffic history remains available independently.

Landed in ConnectionsView, ConnectionGraphData, ConnectionHistory, MainWindow.Connections and preference normalization. Tests cover executable aggregation, complete Properties, pin ordering and limits, restoration, executable restart, PID reuse, missing observations and preference round trips. The smoke renders pins and disconnected pins with both themes, Capture and German minimum-width layouts. The benchmark covers executable rows with and without network monitoring, retained history and five pins. This replaces the earlier Individual/Grouped and expandable-endpoint design; no other departure.
